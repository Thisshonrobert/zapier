## Measurement boundaries and targets

The [master plan](../../../docs/ai-dlq-master-plan.md) specifies initial diagnosis acceptance **80%**, retrieval top-three recall **90%**, and zero evaluated safety violations (Phase 11, lines 266–269; Phase 11A, lines 282–286). The targeted R2 development follow-up revises retrieval Recall@3 to **80%** (line 321); frozen historical configurations retain 90%. Neither threshold is a semantic-correctness certification. No numerical precision or abstention acceptance threshold is specified there. No independent quality acceptance is established by this investigation.

Section recall measures labeled section coverage; precision measures labeled relevance among returned sections on positive questions. No-match abstention has its own negative-control denominator. Incident-family coverage is coarser than section recall. Schema/service diagnosis acceptance, semantic correctness, delivery consistency and provider reliability are separate measurements. Do not substitute one for another or pool the cohorts below.

All current follow-ups use inspected synthetic development material. Old reused held-out questions are diagnostic evidence, not independent validation after reuse; audited labels are agent judgments with human adjudication pending. Direct and incident-projected questions differ in provider eligibility. Fresh held-out artifacts are excluded from this decision and archive scope; their inputs, labels, rationales, scores and reports were not opened for this closeout.

## Remaining quality gap

Production keyword retrieval accepts incidental lexical overlap and often returns generic procedures. Provider restrictions can remove labeled answers before ranking; relaxing access eligibility is not an authorized quality fix. Runbooks cannot recover missing receipts, SDK results, worker fallback credentials or historical execution/configuration facts. Ranking omissions and low labeled precision remain even when coarse family coverage looks complete.

Production diagnosis can cite allowed evidence and passages yet still assert an unsupported defect or recommend correction. Its current schema, membership and disclosure checks do not adjudicate all free-text entailment or consistently separate causal uncertainty from delivery certainty. Existing replay safeguards remain essential; a historical noisy-context proposal was blocked by graph validation, not rendered safe by generation quality. Missing runtime evidence sometimes warrants a bounded hypothesis and investigation, and sometimes blocks delivery/replay conclusions.

Manually scoped repair checks are not autonomous validation. Rejection of bad advice does not produce a useful diagnosis. No observed error within the reviewed scope certifies universal coverage. Independent scope annotation is deferred because no production integration is proposed. Fixing the known retrieval or diagnosis defects does not guarantee the 80% recall/acceptance targets, semantic correctness or readiness.
